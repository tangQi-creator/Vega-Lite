Malaysia Tourism: Arrivals, Spending, and Demographics

This visualisation examines tourism trends in Malaysia from 1995 to 2022, focusing on tourist arrivals, spending patterns by country of origin, and visitor demographic profiles before and after the COVID-19 disruption.

Section 1

Tourist arrivals in Malaysia, 1995–2022

International tourist arrivals to Malaysia grew steadily from 7.5 million in 1995 to a peak of 27.4 million in 2014. Growth was interrupted twice — by the SARS outbreak in 2003 and the Global Financial Crisis in 2008 — before recovering both times. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020–2021 caused a far more severe and prolonged disruption.

Observation

Arrivals in 2021 fell to 134,816 — the lowest recorded figure since modern tourism statistics began. This represents a decline of over 99% from the 2019 level of 26.1 million.

International Tourist Arrivals to Malaysia, 1995–2022

Source: World Bank (ST.INT.ARVL); 2021–2022 from Tourism Malaysia official reports. Key events are annotated.

Impact of COVID-19 on Malaysian Tourism

The sharp decline in 2021 interrupted a tourism growth trend that had continued for almost two decades. Prior disruptions — SARS in 2003 and the Global Financial Crisis in 2008 — caused temporary dips but were followed by strong recoveries. The COVID-19 border closures produced a qualitatively different outcome, with arrivals dropping to near zero for an extended period. The post-pandemic recovery may also shift attention from tourist volume toward tourism value and visitor spending patterns.

Section 2

Tourist spending patterns by country of origin

Average daily expenditure varies considerably across Malaysia's 20 main source markets. Middle Eastern and Western markets tend to spend more per day, while neighbouring ASEAN countries contribute higher visitor volumes but lower per-day spend.

Observation

Saudi Arabia tourists spent an average of RM1,070 per day in 2017 — more than three times the daily spend of Thailand visitors (RM348). This gap highlights the difference between high-volume and high-value tourism markets.

Section 3

Tourist demographic profiles

Occupation and travel companion data from the Departing Visitor Survey provide a demographic profile of Malaysia's inbound tourists. The data covers 2016 and 2017, offering a view of visitor characteristics in the years immediately before the pandemic.

Travel Companion Type (2016)

The majority of tourists arrived with a spouse or family members. Solo travel accounted for approximately 16% of visitors.

Click a year in the legend to highlight it. The Management/Administration category declined from 23.5% in 2016 to 16.3% in 2017, while Housewife and Retired categories increased.

Travel Companion Type: 2016 vs 2017

The proportion travelling with a spouse increased substantially in 2017, while the With Family/Relatives and With Friends categories declined. Source: Departing Visitor Survey, Tourism Malaysia.

Observations on Visitor Demographics

The occupation data indicates that a large share of Malaysia's inbound tourists are from professional and managerial backgrounds — demographic groups that typically have higher disposable income. Travel companion patterns suggest that couple and family travel dominate, which has implications for the types of tourism products and experiences that are most relevant to Malaysia's target markets. Year-on-year shifts between 2016 and 2017 in both occupation and companion categories suggest that visitor profiles are not static and may respond to external factors such as exchange rates, marketing campaigns, or changes in source market demographics.
